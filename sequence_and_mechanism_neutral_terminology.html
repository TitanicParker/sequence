How the sequence became self-reinforcing

A systems view of compression, separation, evidential reassignment, path dependence, pain/distress, treatment interpretation, complaint response, and eventual PRKN/dystonia confirmation.

Clinical sequence Evidential reassignment = information moves out of its original context and is later used with altered evidential weight 2017 → 2026
1 · GP trajectory

The original problem is longitudinal

Gait + initiation difficulty + muscle tension + increased tone + clawing/toes + worsening dexterity + functional deterioration.

The question is not merely “is this Parkinsonism?” It is: what process explains the deteriorating movement disorder and the connected lower-limb history?

↓
2 · First compression

Trajectory becomes a short hospital problem statement

Difficulty initiating movement + stiffness/tone + cogwheeling → assess Parkinsonism.

The omitted remainder does not cease to exist. It becomes something the eventual discharge synthesis must explain after the focused Parkinsonism investigation.

↓
3 · Neurology investigation

Parkinsonism is investigated deeply — and the missing history is partly recovered

  • The long symptom trajectory is reconstructed.
  • The patient’s distinction between foot pain and persistent leg tension is recorded.
  • Neurology independently discovers a dystonic-looking lower-limb gait, described as “NOT FOOT DROP”.
↓
4 · The fork

The newly discovered dystonic sign creates a genuine choice

Reintegrate

Ask whether longstanding tension, foot/toe phenomena and the newly observed dystonic lower limb belong to the same young-onset Parkinsonism/dystonia phenotype.

Separate and close

Complete the Parkinsonism work-up while assigning the residual lower-limb/foot material to another possible mechanism.

↓
5 · Evidential reassignment

The residual material changes meaning as it crosses the fork

Original: corns/callouses and foot pain are distinguished by the patient from persistent leg tension.
↓
Repackaged: “leg symptoms including corns and callouses and burning”.
↓
Patient distinction is given a new evidential function: “markedly divorced” no longer simply describes phenomenology; it helps make two separate disorders look natural.
↓
New destination: “perhaps a secondary peripheral neuropathy”, with alcohol available as a possible contributor.
↓
Genetic effect: the core phenotype points toward PRKN; PARK9 enters specifically through “the neuropathy question”.
↓
6 · What remains available

The record now contains two parallel explanatory tracks

Established track

Parkinsonism → young-onset/genetic reasoning → dopaminergic treatment.

Separate track

Foot/leg pain → possible neuropathy → alcohol/other neuropathy causes.

The dystonic observation remains in the record, but is not made the organising explanation for the lower-limb trajectory.

↓
7 · Eight years of path dependence

New evidence is interpreted inside the partition

Cramping, toe curling and abnormal walking can raise the possibility of OFF dystonia, while the painful foot phenomenon remains on the separate neuropathy pathway.

If dopamine improves pain: Parkinsonism may simply be changing the experience of pain from another cause.

If Lyrica appears to help: that report can be used to reinforce the neuropathic-pain explanation.

↓
8 · Second evidential reassignment — the pain crisis

Relief is detached from the conditions in which it was reported

Actual context: extreme pain + major psychological distress + high-dose Lyrica already present + Sinemet/dopaminergic treatment also present + multiple changing variables.
↓
Patient reports some relief on Lyrica.
↓
Context fades: “I experienced relief while desperate and while several variables were changing” becomes closer to “Lyrica treated the neuropathic pain.”
↓
The report is then used retrospectively as support for the pre-existing neuropathy frame.

The patient’s later clarification: at that level of pain, even nonspecific or transient relief would have felt significant.

↓
9 · Contradictory signal

Dopaminergic timing produces striking relief

Sinemet timing and later Sinemet CR create an opportunity to reopen the original classification:

Was the lower-limb phenomenon itself dopamine-responsive dystonia?

↓
10 · The existing frame absorbs contradictory evidence

The contradictory signal can be explained without reopening the original classification

  • Sinemet can improve the experience of pain whatever its cause → therefore response need not disprove neuropathy.
  • Lyrica had reportedly helped → therefore deterioration after reducing it can be used to support neuropathic pain.
  • The two treatments are clinically entangled, making any later assignment of causal weight difficult to isolate.
↓
11 · Complaint-stage repetition of the same compression

The mechanism occurs again at the level of explanation

Original complaint question: how did an observed dystonic lower-limb sign plus a longstanding trajectory become separated and remain uninvestigated as dystonia while neuropathy became the standing explanation?
↓
Compressed answerable question: why was Sinemet not increased earlier? Was alcohol blamed exclusively? Was neuropathy considered reasonably?
↓
Result: downstream treatment decisions can be explained at length while the upstream classification event remains untouched.
↓
12 · Endpoint

PRKN + Parkinsonism + dystonia

The later genetic result does not need to prove that every painful sensation was dystonia.

Its structural significance is that the disease ultimately identified includes the very phenomenon Neurology had already observed in the lower limb in 2017.

↓
Whole mechanism

Trajectory → compression → investigation → dystonia discovered → non-reintegration → evidential reassignment of the residual symptoms → neuropathy hypothesis → path-dependent interpretation → pain/distress/treatment signals reused inside that frame → contradictory Sinemet response absorbed → complaint compressed in the same manner → original fork remains unanswered.
